## Abstract

Completing a manipulation task does not establish that a robot follows the requested goal rather than a familiar routine. Our proposed **ManipScope** is an evaluation-only diagnostic benchmark for robotic instruction following under changed task requirements. Built using RoboTwin 2.0's simulation resources, it specifies 48 scenes across six task families with explicit source-task links. Policies are trained on unmodified RoboTwin 2.0 demonstrations; we deliberately provide no expert training demonstrations for these held-out scenes, targeting skill reuse under changed requests rather than fitting supplied examples. Task-success and selection metrics measure completion and satisfaction of the requested properties. Controlled instruction and layout changes test whether choices track the request, with future-video and internal-intervention probes where access permits. Evaluations of vision–language–action policies, world-action policies, and agents reveal uneven task performance, partial instruction satisfaction, and correct selection without completion. In 13,200 formal episodes on eleven selection scenes, 84.6% of failures displace a distractor and 3.0% only the target. When the target is reassigned among the candidates on identical initial states, ten checkpoints follow every instruction on 3.9% of 1,000 states, below the 16% of an instruction-blind chooser, although two follow color. The failure recurs on unmodified training tasks and is not explained by missing skills, so formal success in these scenes largely reflects instruction-independent preferences. In controlled checkpoint case studies, individual-trial success can coexist with weak paired instruction correctness; forecasts and execution can agree on the wrong target; and internal interventions change predicted actions without establishing useful goal transfer. These findings show why success, consistency, and sensitivity must each be checked against the requested goal.

## Key findings

- **Failures are wrong choices more often than failed grasps.** Of the 9,587 failures among 13,200 formal episodes on the eleven pick-type selection scenes, 84.6% displace a distractor, 3.0% only the target and 12.4% nothing ([failure decomposition]({{rel}}diagnostics/#where-failures-arise)).
- **Selection is unchanged after the instruction changes.** When the target is reassigned among the candidates on identical initial states, ten checkpoints displace the same object under every instruction on 68% of 1,000 states (an instruction-blind chooser: 33%) and follow every instruction on 3.9% (blind chooser: 16%); LingBot-VA and π0.5 follow color ([target reassignment]({{rel}}diagnostics/#target-reassignment)).
- **Formal success records a preference.** Success is 77.0% where the preferred object is the target and 1.2% where it is not.
- **Not the scene change, not a missing skill.** On unmodified RoboTwin training tasks, five of six verified checkpoints never displace the blue block first when asked to (0/100 runs), and on a size task eight displace the requested largest block first in 1 of 160 runs; nine of ten checkpoints complete at least nine of the ten linked source tasks in 70% or more of episodes ([controls]({{rel}}diagnostics/#controls-training-tasks-and-source-tasks)).
- **Isolated success is not reliable instruction following.** Motus succeeds in 40.0% of individual trials of a paired color study yet selects the requested target under both instructions in 1/40 pairs (π0.5: 29/40); all 77 readable Motus forecasts agree with the object it later contacts, including 46 wrong targets ([controlled study]({{rel}}diagnostics/#controlled-colour-selection-study)).
- **Correct selection without completion.** In the attribute study, 50 of 315 episodes (15.9%) select the correct target first but do not complete the task.

All diagnostics, including the future-video readout and the internal key/value intervention, are released with the code (`diagnostics/` in the [code repository](https://anonymous.4open.science/r/ManipScope)) and run on any registered policy.

## What the benchmark asks

Does a robot satisfy *all* the requirements of an instruction? When asked to put a small red object to the left of a cup, a system may choose a large red object, place the correct object on the wrong side, or fail to grasp it. In a sequence, it may complete the first action but stop before the second. ManipScope evaluates these questions in RoboTwin-derived scenes. The evaluated unit is the complete system that interacts with the robot environment: VLA policies, world-action-model policies, and agent systems that use a language model with tools or controllers. Differences in training, observations, action representations and execution support remain part of the comparison and are documented rather than equalised.

## What ManipScope provides

- **48 evaluation-only scenes** in six task families (D1–D6), each linked to the RoboTwin 2.0 source tasks it derives from, with an instruction pool, initial-state distribution, target and distractor definitions, step limit and terminal success rule. No expert demonstrations for training on the evaluation scenes are released.
- **A frozen protocol** (`formal48-v1`): 100 expert-checked initial states per scene that fix both layout and instruction, so every system faces the identical episode list. The corresponding seeds are retained privately by the organizers to protect public-evaluation fairness; protocol hashes fail validation if scenes, instruction pools or step limits drift.
- **Measurements beyond final success**: task success is scored as correct selection × goal completion. An [attribute study]({{rel}}diagnostics/#attribute-selection-and-completion-on-the-formal-tasks) measures selection at the first loaded interaction across 315 episodes (five policies and two agents on nine attribute tasks): 50 of 315 trials select the correct target but fail to finish. Subtask-completion (individual-stage and valid-prefix) rates are defined and remain to be exported.
- **[Language-following diagnostics]({{rel}}diagnostics/)** that run on any policy: selection events and gripper-state contact classes, failure decomposition, target reassignment on frozen initial states with an instruction-blind reference, its two controls (unmodified training tasks, source tasks) and first-decision sensitivity; plus future-video readouts where a system predicts images and internal interventions where representations are accessible.
- **Separate policy and agent leaderboards** with a matched-seed subset (first 5 seeds per scene, 240 episodes) for a limited comparison between the two groups, plus a per-checkpoint record of [training provenance and enabled inference modules]({{rel}}policies/#provenance).
- **Adapters** for twelve public checkpoints (joint- and end-effector-controlled) plus an externally hosted agent bridge, each running in its own environment behind an HTTP contract; per-episode metadata, stage progress, head-camera video and provenance records for reporting.

## Agent evaluation update

The [agent leaderboard]({{rel}}leaderboard/#agents) now includes **GPT-6.2 Astra, Gemini 4 Argon and Opus 5.5**, alongside the original GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 3.8 Flash evaluations: five agents and twelve learned policies in total. Results are integrated into the comparison tables; [request settings and budget adjustments]({{rel}}leaderboard/#agents-config) document the model-specific configurations.
